# Toolbelt content calendar

**Written 2026-07-12.** Twenty long-tail post briefs, ordered by what I would write next.

The three at the top of the list are already written (`/blog/`). The remaining twenty are
briefed here: target keyword, the search intent behind it, an outline, and — the part that
usually gets skipped and is the reason a blog does nothing for a site — **which internal
pages each post must link to.**

## How to use this

A post is worth writing when it satisfies all three of these. If it fails one, it is a
blog post that will sit there getting forty visits a year.

1. **Someone is actually searching for it.** Not "would find it interesting" — typing it.
2. **We can say something a generic finance blog cannot.** We have ten trade pages of
   specifics, a rate calculator and an app that works on job sites. If a post could have
   been written by anyone about anything, it will rank like it.
3. **It has somewhere to send the reader.** Every post links to at least one **trade page**
   and one **template/tool page**, contextually — inside a sentence where the link is the
   genuinely useful next click, not in a "related posts" box at the bottom that nobody has
   ever clicked in the history of the web.

**Cadence:** one a week is plenty. Two a month, done properly, beats four thin ones — and
thin posts do not just fail, they drag the domain down with them.

**Dates:** pin the real publication date in the `BlogPosting` schema. All five original
posts shipped claiming `datePublished: 2025-01-20` — a year wrong and identical across all
five, which is exactly the pattern that tells a search engine the dates are fiction.

## Deliberately not on this list

- **"10 best power drills."** Affiliate-bait, no relationship to invoicing, and it would tell Google this site is about tools rather than getting paid. Topical authority is built by being narrow.
- **Anything giving specific legal or tax advice.** We are allowed to explain what a mechanic's lien *is*; we are not allowed to tell someone how to file one. Every post that goes near law or tax carries a caveat and a link to the real authority.
- **AI-spun variations of the posts above** ("how to write an invoice for plumbers", "…for electricians", "…for roofers"). That is the doorway-page pattern the trade pages already handle properly, and doing it in the blog too would be pushing our luck with a policy we have so far respected.
